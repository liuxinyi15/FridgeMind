# FridgeMind V0.1 — Project Brief

## Design challenge

FridgeMind explores how a proactive, personalised food-planning system might help adults living alone use existing ingredients, decide what to cook, and plan shopping with less administration while retaining control.

The V0.1 outcome is an HCI functional demo, not a validated commercial product. It demonstrates one coherent loop: decide what to cook → inspect stock → scale a recipe → identify gaps → shop → cook → minimally correct inventory → receive an explainable food-rescue opportunity.

## Evidence labels

- **Research evidence:** reported aggregate survey results supplied to the project.
- **Design hypothesis:** an interaction that appears worth testing but is not proven effective.
- **Implementation decision:** a scoped technical/product choice for this prototype.
- **Future validation needed:** a question to test with target participants.

## Target users and demo persona

The intended target is adults living alone / single-person households. Alex is a **demo scenario persona for prototype walkthrough only**, not an interview-derived persona. Alex cooks 3–4 times weekly, shops 1–2 times weekly, sometimes cooks two portions, prefers quick meals, usually eats weekday lunch away from home, and enables actionable reminders.

## Success criteria

The linked state between inventory, recipes, servings, shopping and cooking works; interventions are explainable and controllable; uncertainty and safety are visible; the 390px experience has no horizontal overflow; and evidence can be reproduced for assessment.
