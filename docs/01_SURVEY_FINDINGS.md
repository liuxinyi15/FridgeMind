# Survey Findings

## Interpretation boundary

The survey has 115 responses: 111/115 were aged 18+ and consented; 52/115 reported living alone and 63/115 did not. Later summaries still use all 115 responses. **The available survey summary contains responses from both people living alone and people not currently living alone. Unless target-group-filtered raw data is later available, the findings should be treated as directional evidence rather than population-specific conclusions.** No statistic below is presented as a conclusion about the 52 people living alone.

## Problems in the previous four weeks

- Duplicate purchase — 0: 60.87%; 1: 26.96%; 2–3: 8.70%.
- Forgotten ingredients — 0: 46.96%; 1: 35.65%; 2–3: 15.65%.
- Oversized packages — 0: 42.61%; 1: 33.04%; 2–3: 20.00%; 4+: 3.48%.
- Changed plans — 0: 28.70%; 1: 31.30%; 2–3: 31.30%; 4+: 6.09%.
- Discarded intended food — 0: 28.70%; 1: 38.26%; 2–3: 20.87%; 4+: 10.43%.

This suggests testing plan changes, leftovers/use-soon food, portion/package mismatch and low-effort stock awareness.

## Food waste reasons

Suspected/observed spoilage 57.39%; bought/cooked too much 38.26%; forgot food 25.22%; plans changed 22.61%; did not know how to use leftovers 6.96%. A reminder should therefore offer an action, but **food safety takes priority over waste reduction**; the prototype never declares uncertain food safe.

## Recipes

- Online recipe portions: suitable 60.87%; unsuitable 39.13%.
- Last saved recipe: cooked as written 25.22%; cooked with scaling/substitution 26.96%; intends to cook 19.13%; decided not to 6.96%; did not save 21.74%.
- Among 55 answering why a recipe was not cooked: too little time/too complex 47.27%; forgot/changed mind 40.00%; missing ingredients 34.55%; uncertain stock 12.73%; unsuitable portions 9.09%; leftover purchase concern 9.09%.

Recommendation should test more than ingredient coverage: use-soon coverage, effort/time, missing count and portion fit are included.

## Most difficult stage

Deciding what to eat 62.61%; using perishable food in time 27.83%; deciding what/how much to buy 26.09%; using food after plans change 16.52%; package size 15.65%; scaling recipes 14.78%; remembering stock 8.70%; no major difficulty 13.91%. This is why Today starts with “What should I cook?” rather than an inventory dashboard.

## Intervention, control and input

- Spinach intervention preference: simple reminder 32.17%; reminder + recipe 23.48%; reminder + storage/planning actions 24.35%; in-app only 4.35%; none 15.65%.
- Desired control: correct information 42.61%; see why 33.04%; quiet hours 33.04%; snooze 29.57%; ignore 18.26%; disable reminder type 8.70%; undo automatic update 6.96%.
- Input preference: photo 48.70%; manual/list 36.52%; receipt 30.43%; only selected items 26.96%; unwilling 13.91%; unsure 8.70%.
- Post-cooking confirmation: every time 19.13%; only when uncertain 15.65%; when remembered 34.78%; unwilling 15.65%; unsure 14.78%.
- Habit inference: ask before changing 33.91%; show only 26.96%; apply with undo 22.61%; no inference 9.57%; unsure 6.96%.

These findings motivate opt-in intervention modes, explain/snooze/dismiss/correct controls, demo photo recognition with review, “correction over administration”, and the pattern **Suggest → Explain → Confirm → Apply**. All are hypotheses for usability testing.
